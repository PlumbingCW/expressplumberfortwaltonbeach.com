What a proper water heater installation includes

A good installation is more than hooking up pipes. The unit needs a stable base or pan, shutoff valves on the supply, a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe that runs to a safe spot, correct venting for combustion gases and a gas or electrical connection that matches the unit.

Florida homes follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which sets the rules for water heater installations. Permit requirements vary, so ask the Fort Walton Beach building department about your address. A plumber who does the work every week knows what the inspector expects.

Every install has the same basic sequence, whether you are replacing an old unit or adding a new one.

The plumber checks the location, fuel, venting and demand.

2

The area is cleared, a pan is placed and shutoff valves are checked.

3

Set and connect

Water, gas or electric and venting are connected to code.

4

Test and explain

The unit is filled, checked for leaks and combustion safety, and you are shown the shutoff and settings.

Keep the owner manual and registration paperwork. Many manufacturers tie warranties to registration.

Sizing: how much hot water do you really need

Tank heaters are rated by first hour rating, which tells you how much hot water the heater can deliver in a busy hour. Tankless models are rated by flow in gallons per minute at a given temperature rise. Both depend on household size and the number of fixtures that run together.

A family that showers in the morning needs a different unit from a couple with one bathroom. A plumber will ask about the number of people, bathrooms and big hot water appliances. Oversizing wastes money, and undersizing leaves you with cold showers.

Installing in a coastal Florida home

Garages, utility closets and covered porches are common water heater spots on the coast. Moisture and salt air can shorten the life of fittings and exhaust components, so a protected location and good drainage matter. A drain pan with a line to a safe point is a smart addition, especially above finished space.

New construction in nearby Navarre and Destin often runs plumbing and venting through slab and framing that must be coordinated with the builder. If you are building, see our plumbing installation page. For an existing home with a failing unit, water heater replacement is the better page to read.

Installation, permits and the code

Installing a heater is regulated work. The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), covers the relief valve, drain and venting, and the National Electrical Code (NEC) covers the wiring on an electric unit.

When to call us: before you buy, so the fuel, venting and space can be checked.

Water heater installation questions

A tank is simpler and costs less up front. Tankless saves space and can supply hot water continuously, but needs the right gas or power. The best choice depends on your home and budget.

Permit rules vary by location and project. Ask the Fort Walton Beach building department about your address, and we can describe the planned work so you know what to ask.

Many installs take a few hours. Moving the heater, adding venting or changing fuel can take longer.
