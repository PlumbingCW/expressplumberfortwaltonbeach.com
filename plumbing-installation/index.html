What we install

Most installation calls fall into a few groups. Fixture installs include sinks, faucets, toilets, tubs, showers and kitchen additions. Appliance hookups include dishwashers, ice makers and washers. Remodel work moves or adds supply and drain lines when a layout changes.

New construction work is split into rough-in, when pipes go in before walls close, and finish, when fixtures are set. Plumbing is coordinated with the builder and the inspector, and mistakes at the rough-in stage are costly to fix.

Plumbing for new homes in the Fort Walton Beach area

Growth in Navarre and the outer ring has brought new subdivisions, and Destin and Okaloosa Island see steady remodels of older condos and vacation homes. New homes are built to the Florida Building Code, Plumbing. Rough-in inspections happen before walls close, and permit rules vary, so builders confirm what applies with the local building department.

For a remodel of an older house, the first step is finding out what pipe is in the walls and whether the drains and vents can handle the new layout. If the supply is old galvanized, repiping at the same time can save opening walls twice.

Gas line work: safety first

Gas lines are not a do-it-yourself job. If you smell gas, leave the building, then call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. Do not turn on lights or use a phone inside.

A gas line for a range, dryer, water heater or fireplace must be sized and pressure tested, and a licensed professional should do the work. We do not promise a response time for gas leaks. Safety comes first, and the gas company has the right equipment to make the area safe.

Sump and ejector pumps

Most homes along the coast do not have sump pits, but some low lots and basements or lower levels near bayous may use sump or ejector pumps to move water or waste uphill. A pump that fails during a heavy rain can flood a lower level quickly. A check valve, a working float and a backup power plan are the usual safeguards.

Good planning shortens the job.

3

4

Test and clean up

The system is pressure checked, run and tested for leaks, then the area is cleaned.

Have fixtures on site before the plumber arrives. Missing parts are the most common cause of delay.

Permits for new plumbing

New piping and fixtures are built to the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and a permit and inspection may be required. A licensed contractor can be checked on MyFloridaLicense.com.

When to call us: while the walls are still open, so the rough-in can be checked before it is covered.
