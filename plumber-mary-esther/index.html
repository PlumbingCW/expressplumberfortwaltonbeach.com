Older homes, older pipes: what Mary Esther plumbing looks like

Mary Esther has a mix of postwar houses and newer infill. Many are single-story homes on concrete slabs, which means supply lines can run under the floor and drains are buried in the slab. That layout is why slab leaks, cast iron drain failures and corroded galvanized lines come up so often in older neighborhoods.

If a house was built in the middle of the last century, it may still have original drain pipe. Cast iron rusts from the inside and narrows over time, and clay or early plastic pipes can crack. A camera inspection is the best way to see what is going on, and our sewer inspection page explains the process.

The slab leak question

Slab leaks are the problem most associated with Mary Esther homes. Warning signs include a spinning meter, a damp spot on the floor, a sudden rise in the water bill and the sound of water with everything off. The fix depends on how many leaks there are and where they sit. Our slab leak repair page describes the options, and repiping is the next step if the pipe is failing everywhere.
