Where Ferry Park sits

According to the City of Fort Walton Beach's November 2023 Ferry Park Neighborhood Traffic Study, the neighborhood sits north of Hollywood Boulevard and east of State Road 85, which locals know as Eglin Parkway. Streets the study discusses include Yacht Club Drive NE, Nebraska Avenue NE, Hughes Street NE, Ferry Road NE, Smith Drive NE and Oregon Drive NE.

The neighborhood also holds Ferry Park, a city park with a walking path, a disc golf course and tennis courts, and Elliott Point Elementary School. The study classes most of its roads as suburban residential. It does not list every boundary, so we describe the area by these anchor streets and do not draw an exact line.

Homes in the neighborhood and what that means for plumbing

The city study does not give a housing age or a pipe material. One local real estate guide describes many Ferry Park homes as built from the 1950s through the 1970s, which we could not confirm with county records. Age alone does not tell you what is inside your walls. A house from any decade can have copper, PEX, CPVC or older metal supply lines, and a drain made of several materials after past repairs.

The practical step is to look and not guess. A camera look at the main drain and a check of the visible supply lines at the heater, the meter and under the sinks show what you actually have. Our sewer camera inspection page explains that visit.

Plumbing calls homeowners ask about

These are general plumbing topics for houses. They are not claims about a problem that is common in Ferry Park.

Slow or blocked drains

A kitchen sink that drains slowly, a tub that holds water or a floor drain that backs up. See drain cleaning.

Water heater trouble

A tank that runs out quickly, leaks at the base or makes popping sounds. See water heater repair and replacement.

Hidden leaks

A water bill that climbs with no change in use, or a damp spot on a wall or floor. See leak detection.

Toilets and faucets

Running toilets, weak flushes and dripping faucets. See toilet repair and faucet repair.

A short checklist before you call

Having a few facts ready makes the first conversation faster and more useful.

1

Find the main shutoff

Know where the main water valve is and that it turns.

2

Note when it started

A sudden change points to a different cause than a slow one.

3

Check other fixtures

If several drains act up together, the main line is more likely than a single trap.

4

Take photos or a meter reading

A picture of the damp spot or a meter reading with everything off helps.

5

Skip chemical drain cleaners

They can sit in the trap and are a hazard for whoever opens the drain next.

What we verified and what we did not

We verified the location and streets above from the City of Fort Walton Beach study. We did not confirm which water and sewer utility serves each address. The City of Fort Walton Beach describes its supply as groundwater from deep wells in the Floridan Aquifer in its 2020 water quality report, but whether your street is on city service depends on the address, so check your bill.

We do not state water hardness figures or claim a plumbing problem unique to this neighborhood. If you want to understand your own water, test your tap and ask your utility for its annual report.

Questions from Ferry Park

The City of Fort Walton Beach places the Ferry Park neighborhood north of Hollywood Boulevard and east of Eglin Parkway. Boundaries beyond that are not listed in the city study.

We have no data showing a problem unique to Ferry Park, and we do not claim one. Problems depend on the age, materials and use of each home.

Look at the visible supply lines at the water heater and under the sinks, and ask for a camera look at the main drain. Our sewer inspection page explains the camera visit.

Close the main water valve. If it will not turn, call your water utility for the street shutoff and then call us to discuss repair. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or your gas utility first.
