When a water heater problem is an emergency

A water heater is an emergency when water is pooling around it, when you smell gas or when the tank is making loud popping or rumbling sounds with heat. The 24/7 line is open and a plumber can talk you through the safe steps.

If you smell gas, leave the house, then call the gas utility or 911 before calling us. Do not flip switches or light anything. For a leak, follow the steps below.

1

Turn off the power

Switch off the breaker for an electric unit or set a gas unit to off or pilot.

2

Close the cold water supply

The valve on the cold inlet pipe stops water from refilling the tank.

3

Contain the water

Use towels and a bucket, and move belongings away from the base.

4

Do not drain it yet

Wait for the plumber unless the water is dangerously hot or flooding.

A leak from the top, from a fitting, or from the relief valve may be repairable. Water coming from the bottom of the tank usually means the tank has failed.

Repairs that are worth doing

Thermostats, heating elements, thermocouples, igniters, relief valves and drain valves are all repairable parts. A tank that is under about ten years old and in good shape is usually worth repairing, because the repair cost is small compared with a new heater.

Sediment flushing is another cost-effective fix. Minerals and debris settle at the bottom of the tank and force the heater to work harder. A flush can restore performance and reduce noise. If you are not sure whether to repair or replace, our page on water heater replacement sets out how to decide.

Why a water heater leak usually means replacement

A leak from the tank body means the steel liner has corroded through. That cannot be patched. The leak that looks like a failed tank may actually be a loose fitting, a dripping relief valve or condensation, so a plumber checks before recommending a new unit.

A drain pan under the heater is a worthwhile safeguard in a home. It catches slow leaks and can run to a drain line. If yours has no pan and the heater sits above living space, ask about adding one.

Electric, gas and hybrid units

Gas heaters need regular attention to the burner, the venting and the gas connection. Electric heaters need working elements and thermostats. Hybrid heat pump models use a compressor and fan and have extra parts to service, including condensate drains and air filters.

Near the Sound and the Gulf, salt air can speed up corrosion of flue pipes, fittings and the outer jacket. A heater in a garage or on a covered porch will show that wear sooner than one in a conditioned closet. If yours sits outdoors or in a damp garage, a yearly look at the fittings and venting is a sensible habit.

Water heater safety and the code

A water heater has to be installed to the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), including a relief valve and drain. An electric unit is wired under the National Electrical Code (NEC).

What to do before we arrive: switch the heater off at the breaker or gas control, close the cold supply if it is leaking and keep the area around it clear.
