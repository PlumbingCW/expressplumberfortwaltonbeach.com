Emergency Slab Leak Repair in Fort Walton Beach, FL

A slab leak is a break or pinhole in a water line running beneath or inside the concrete foundation of a house. Many homes in Fort Walton Beach sit on slabs, and when a line there fails the signs show up as warm floors, a spinning meter or a bill that rises without explanation.

A suspected slab leak: what to do now

A slab leak is an emergency when you can hear water running with everything off, when a floor feels hot or when water is surfacing in a room. The 24/7 line is open, and these steps buy time.

Close the main valve

This stops the flow while you decide next steps. Locate it before you need it.

Open a faucet downstairs

It relieves pressure and confirms the water is really off at the main.

Stay off wet flooring

Wet tile, wood and carpet can be slippery and may hide electrical hazards.

Note where the warm spot is

Tell the dispatcher the rooms and the floor type.

If the water heater is not getting water, switch it off at the breaker or set gas to pilot so the tank does not run dry while heating.

How a slab leak is confirmed

Plumbers do not guess at a slab leak. They check the water meter, shut off supply to isolate sections and use acoustic equipment on the floor to hear the leak. Thermal imaging can show a hot water line leaking beneath the surface.

Once the spot is mapped, the plumber explains the choices. A small, localized leak can sometimes be repaired through a small opening in the slab. A line with several leaks or one that is old and brittle may be better abandoned and rerouted through the walls or attic.

Repair choices for a slab leak

Each choice trades cost, disruption and how long the fix is likely to last.

OptionHow it worksTradeoff
Spot repairOpening the slab above the leak and replacing the sectionFast for one leak, but other weak spots remain.
RerouteNew lines run through the attic or walls and the old line is cappedLess slab damage and a longer-lasting fix, with more wall work.
Epoxy liningA coating is applied inside the pipeLimited use and not for every pipe type.
RepipeReplacing the whole supply systemBest when the pipe is failing everywhere.

Ask the plumber to show you the findings from the leak test before you pick an option.

Signs of a leak beneath the slab

Several clues together are a strong hint that the leak is under the floor.

Hot spots on the floor

A hot water line is leaking and warming the concrete.

Sound of running water

Water moves in a line when all fixtures are off.

Meter moves with everything off

Water is leaving the system somewhere.

Cracks or damp edges along baseboards

Water is moving through the slab or soil.

Low pressure at fixtures

The line is losing flow underground.

Rule out a running toilet and an irrigation leak first, because they cause the same meter movement.

Why slab leaks happen in this area

Slab lines can fail from corrosion, from movement as the soil settles and from the friction of pipe rubbing on concrete. Copper pipe in older homes can develop pinholes, and the soil on the coast can be corrosive to buried metal. Hot water lines tend to fail first because heat expands and stresses the pipe.

If your home has copper under the slab and has started to show pinholes in other places, a repiping project may be a better investment than repeated spot repairs. For other leaks in the house, our leak detection service can trace the source.

What affects the cost of slab leak repair

Slab leaks, safety and the code

Piping under a slab has to meet the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and a repair or reroute may need a permit. Water near wiring is a hazard under the National Electrical Code (NEC) as well.

What to do while you wait: turn the main valve off, keep power off to wet areas and document warm floors and meter readings.
