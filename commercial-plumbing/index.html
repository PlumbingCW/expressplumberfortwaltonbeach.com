When a business plumbing failure is urgent

A kitchen that cannot drain or a restroom with no water usually means closing for the day. Our 24/7 line is open for those calls. A few steps limit the loss while a plumber is on the way.

Stop the water source

Close the main valve or the valve to the affected fixture.

Block off the area

Keep customers and staff away from wet floors and backed-up drains.

Move stock and equipment

Raise cartons and electronics off wet floors.

Document the damage

Take photos for the insurer or landlord.

If you rent the space, tell your landlord or property manager as well. The lease decides who is responsible.

The businesses we serve

Restaurants and cafes need grease management and fast drain service. Short-term rentals and hotels need quick turnaround between guests. Retail shops and offices need working restrooms and hot water. Small multi-unit buildings need leak tracing that does not disturb every tenant.

Fort Walton Beach has a mix of strip centers along US 98, small shops downtown and rentals on Okaloosa Island across Brooks Bridge. Each has its own plumbing history, and we start by finding out what is already in the building before suggesting a fix. We do not service fire sprinkler systems, and we send you to a specialist if your issue falls outside plumbing.

Grease traps, backflow and storm drains

A grease trap or interceptor catches fat before it reaches the sewer. It needs regular pump out and inspection, and a neglected trap will back up into the kitchen. Our crews can clean and check the trap and advise on how often your kitchen should do it.

Backflow prevention assemblies protect the public water supply from contamination. They are tested on a schedule, and the utility usually sets the rules. We can test and report, and repair or replace a failed assembly.

Storm drains and catch basins on a commercial lot can fill with sand, leaves and debris, and flooding follows in a heavy Gulf Coast rain. Keeping them clear before hurricane season is practical. For clogged storm and floor drains, the equipment used is similar to our sewer line cleaning service.

A realistic word on commercial leak detection

Finding a leak in a large commercial building is harder than finding one in a house. Pipes run through ceilings, shared walls and slabs, and testing may need the building to be shut off in sections. We use the same leak detection methods and tell you honestly whether we can isolate the problem or whether a specialist is better placed.

That honesty saves time. We would rather say a job is outside our scope than start work that cannot succeed.

Commercial work and the code

Commercial plumbing is built under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and grease and backflow rules can add requirements. Your local building department and utility decide what applies to your site.

What to do before we arrive: close the nearest shutoff, keep customers out of wet areas and note which fixtures are affected.
