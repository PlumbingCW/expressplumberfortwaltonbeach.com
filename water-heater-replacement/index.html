A failed tank: what to do now

A tank that has split can release dozens of gallons in minutes. The 24/7 line is open at night and on weekends, and a few steps limit the damage before the plumber arrives.

Close the cold supply valve

Find the valve on the pipe feeding the top of the tank and turn it off.

Cut power or gas

Switch off the breaker or turn the gas control to off.

Move belongings

Pull items away from the base and from the flooded area.

Call the line

Tell us the fuel type and the size of the old tank if you know it.

Do not stand in standing water near an electric heater. Turn off the breaker first.

Choosing the replacement unit

Most homes replace like for like, with the same fuel and tank size. That is the simplest path because the gas line, venting and electrical supply are already in place. A larger tank helps a growing household, and a different fuel needs new connections.

Some homeowners use a failure as a reason to switch to a tankless or hybrid heat pump heater. Those options cost more up front and may save on running costs, but they need the right power or gas, venting and space. Our tankless water heater page explains what each option involves. If you are planning a swap in advance, water heater installation covers the new-install side.

What a replacement visit includes

A replacement day follows a set routine.

1

Drain and disconnect

The old tank is drained and the water, gas or electrical lines are disconnected.

2

Remove and set the new unit

The old heater is carried out and the new one is positioned, often on a pan.

3

Reconnect and vent

Supply lines, the relief valve, the discharge pipe and venting are connected.

4

Fill and test

The tank is filled, air is purged and the heater is started and tested for leaks and safe operation.

Ask whether permits apply to your address. Permit rules vary, so check with the Fort Walton Beach building department or your county.

Local factors that affect replacement

Many Fort Walton Beach garages and utility closets hold the water heater, and coastal air can corrode jackets, flue pipes and fittings faster than inland weather. If your heater sits on a slab in an exposed garage, a new unit raised slightly on a stand or pan will resist moisture better.

During hurricane season, which runs from June 1 through November 30, it is worth knowing where your main shutoff is, and having the heater serviced beforehand if it is old. A heater that fails right after a storm is easier to handle when you already know where the shutoffs are.

Codes that apply to a new heater

A replacement heater is set up under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and the local building department may ask for a permit. Electric units follow the National Electrical Code (NEC).
