Emergency Leak Detection in Fort Walton Beach, FL

Leak detection finds where water is escaping from a pipe you cannot see, using listening gear, thermal imaging and pressure tests instead of a sledgehammer. The usual clues are a meter that keeps moving, a warm patch on the floor or a wet stain on a ceiling.

When a hidden leak is an emergency

Water that is damaging a ceiling, soaking a wall or running under a floor is urgent, and the 24/7 line is open for exactly that. While you wait for a plumber, a few steps limit the damage.

Shut off the main valve

Turn the main water valve to off. Know where it is before you need it.

Switch off nearby power

If water is near outlets or a light fixture, turn off that circuit at the panel and stay clear.

Catch and contain

Use buckets under a ceiling drip and towels at the base of wet walls.

Photograph everything

Take pictures of stains and damage for an insurance claim.

Poking a hole in a bulging ceiling can release the trapped water in one go. Place a bucket under it first if you must.

Meter still running: the two-hour test

A water meter that moves when nothing is using water is one of the clearest signs of a leak. You can check it yourself, and it is a good way to confirm the problem before you call. This is also the most useful test when a water bill jumps without explanation.

1

Turn everything off

Close every tap and stop the dishwasher, washer and ice maker.

2

Read the meter

Write down the number and note the small leak indicator dial.

3

Wait two hours

Use no water during that time.

4

Read it again

If the number moved, water is escaping somewhere on your side of the meter.

If the indicator is spinning while every fixture is off, the leak could be in the yard, under the slab or in a running toilet. A toilet flapper that does not seal is the most common cause, and it is worth checking before paying for a locate.

How we pinpoint the leak

The plumber starts with a pressure test to confirm that the system is losing water, then narrows it down. Acoustic listening equipment picks up the hiss of water escaping from a pressurized line. Thermal imaging shows the temperature pattern of a hot water line under a floor or inside a wall. A moisture meter checks drywall and framing to define the wet area.

Once the spot is found, the opening can be small. A section of wall, a square of tile or a short trench is enough for most repairs. For a leak under the house or yard, see our pages on slab leak repair and water line repair.

Leak symptoms and where they point

Most callers start with a symptom and not the word leak. Here are the common ones and what they suggest.

High water bill with no change in use

A constant slow leak in a pipe, toilet or irrigation line.

Warm spot on the floor

A hot water line leaking under the slab.

Ceiling stain or bubble

A supply line, drain or air handler leak above.

Musty smell and peeling paint

Moisture inside a wall.

Low water pressure

A broken pipe is losing flow before it reaches the fixtures.

Soggy yard patch

A leak in the buried water service line.

Hot spots on a floor and sudden bill jumps are the signs that deserve the quickest attention.

Ceiling, wall, crawlspace and yard leaks

Ceiling and wall leaks usually come from a supply line, a drain connection or an air conditioner condensate line. Water travels along framing, so the stain is often not directly below the source. Thermal imaging and a moisture meter trace the path back to the actual leak.

Homes on pier foundations and some older houses have crawlspaces where supply and drain lines are exposed. Leaks there can run for months because nobody looks, and the damp air promotes rot and mold. Under-house leaks are found by listening and by inspecting the lines directly.

In the yard, a leak between the meter box and the house is a buried service line problem. Irrigation systems are a frequent source of unexplained usage, because a stuck zone valve or broken head can run for days. We check them with a shutoff test on the sprinkler supply.

Pool and spa leaks are a separate specialty and require pool equipment. If water loss is only at the pool, a pool service company is the right first call.

Safety steps and the code

Supply piping inside a home falls under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). A hidden leak near wiring is also an electrical risk, so treat wet outlets under the National Electrical Code (NEC) as a hazard and not as a plumbing detail.

What to do before we arrive: shut off the main valve if you can, read the meter, stay away from wet outlets and photograph any damage.
