Water line repair fixes the buried pipe that carries water from the street meter to your house. When it leaks or breaks, pressure drops, the yard gets wet and the water bill climbs.

Service line leak: what to do first

A broken water service line is urgent because it runs at full street pressure. Water may surface in the yard or run unseen. Our line is open around the clock, and the steps below limit the loss.

Close the valve at the house

The main shutoff near where the line enters stops flow from the street side only if the break is past it.

Look for the meter box

Note whether the dial is still spinning after the valve is closed.

Keep clear of pooling water

Soft ground around a washout can collapse.

Call before digging

Dialing 811 before any digging marks buried utilities.

If the meter spins with the house valve closed, the leak is between the meter and the valve. Tell the dispatcher.

Where the service line runs

The service line starts at the water meter, usually near the street, and runs underground to the house. Its material depends on when the house was built. Older lines may be galvanized steel or copper, and newer ones are commonly PEX or polyethylene. The utility owns the meter and the connection to the main. The homeowner generally owns the pipe from the meter to the house.

In the Fort Walton Beach area, water comes from different providers depending on the address. The City of Fort Walton Beach publishes an annual water quality report and describes its supply as groundwater from deep wells in the Floridan Aquifer. Okaloosa County Water and Sewer serves areas around the city, and your bill shows which utility is yours.

The right call depends on the pipe material, the number of leaks and what lies above it.

Ask your utility how the connection at the meter is handled before work starts so the repair scope is clear.

Low pressure and a wet yard

Low water pressure is a symptom, not a diagnosis. A leak, a partly closed valve, a failing pressure regulator or a corroded galvanized line can all cause it. A plumber checks pressure at a hose bib, tests the regulator and then looks at the service line.

A soggy patch in the yard with no rain is a strong clue. Water from a broken line follows the path of least resistance and can surface far from the break. A leak detection visit locates the actual spot so the excavation is small.

Signs your water line is failing

These signs often show up before the line fails completely.

Meter moves with everything off

Water is leaving after the meter.

Pressure drop at every tap

The line is losing flow or is partly restricted.

Wet, sunken or unusually green patch

Water is escaping and soaking the soil.

Sound of water in the yard

A pressurized leak underground.

Rising water bill

A hidden leak is adding to the usage.

Compare a recent bill to the same month last year to see how large the change really is.

Water lines, meters and the code

The line from the meter to the house is built and tested under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and the utility owns the meter side. Dig safely by checking utility locates first.

What to do while you wait: shut the house valve, note the meter reading and mark any wet ground.
