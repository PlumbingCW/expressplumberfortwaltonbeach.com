Disposal trouble: what to do first

A disposal that leaks or floods the sink is urgent, and the 24/7 line is open for it. If the unit hums but does not spin, the cause is usually a jam, and a few safe steps may clear it.

1

Turn off the switch and unplug it

Never put your hand in a disposal, even when it is off.

2

Use the hex key

Many units have a socket on the bottom for an Allen wrench. Work it back and forth to free the jam.

3

Press the reset button

A red button on the bottom pops out when the motor overheats. Wait a few minutes and press it.

4

Look for leaks

Check the sink flange, the drain connections and the body for drips.

If water is pooling under the sink, close the supply valves and call. A disposal leak is not a do-it-yourself fix.

Symptoms and what they point to

Disposals fail in a few predictable ways.

A cracked body cannot be repaired. Replacement is the only fix for a leak from the body.

Repair or replace a garbage disposal

A disposal that jams occasionally or has a loose mount is worth repairing. One that leaks from the body, is rusted or has a motor that trips repeatedly is usually near the end of its life. Many disposals last roughly a decade, depending on use.

A replacement is an inexpensive way to restore a kitchen. A new unit with a stronger motor handles tougher scraps, but it is still not a trash can. We also install disposals where none existed, which includes new wiring and drain adjustments.

What a disposal should and should not handle

A disposal is built for small, soft food scraps. Fibrous peels, bones, coffee grounds, grease and starchy foods such as rice and pasta are what lead to jams and clogs. In a Gulf Coast kitchen, shrimp and crab shells and fish skin do not belong in the drain.

Run cold water during use and for several seconds after, so scraps are flushed down the line. If the kitchen drain keeps clogging even with careful use, the line itself may be coated with grease. In that case hydro jetting cleans the pipe and a drain cleaning visit is the first step.

Disposal safety first

A disposal is wired to household power and drains through a trap that must meet the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). The wiring side follows the National Electrical Code (NEC).

What to do before we arrive: switch the disposal off at the wall, never put a hand in the chamber and keep the sink empty.
