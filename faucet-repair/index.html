Emergency Faucet Repair in Fort Walton Beach, FL

Faucet repair fixes drips, leaks at the handle, weak flow and valves that will not shut off. It also covers the shutoff valves under sinks and the shower and tub valves that sit behind the wall.

When a faucet or valve cannot wait

A drip can wait for a daytime visit, but a valve that will not close or a supply line spraying water cannot. The 24/7 line takes those calls, and these steps keep the damage small.

Close the valve under the sink

Turn the small oval handles clockwise. If they will not move, go to the main.

Shut off the main valve

This stops all water to the house when a local valve fails.

Open another tap to drain

It relieves pressure and confirms the water is off.

Dry the area

Wipe cabinets and floors to prevent swelling and mold.

A shutoff valve that is stuck is a sign that it needs replacing. Do not force it with a wrench, because a corroded stem can snap.

Why faucets drip and leak

A drip usually comes from a worn rubber washer, an O-ring or a cartridge, depending on the faucet type. Compression faucets with two handles use washers that wear against a metal seat. Cartridge and ball faucets use internal parts that crack or stiffen. Ceramic disc models use hardened discs that can pit.

A drip that is ignored wastes water and stains the sink, and over time it can wear the seat so that a simple washer swap no longer works. Fixing it early costs less than waiting.

Older faucets can be hard to find parts for, so a replacement fixture is sometimes the better choice.

Bathtub, shower and valve leaks

A bathtub faucet that drips or a shower that will not shut off completely usually points to a worn cartridge or valve behind the wall. A showerhead that leaks at the arm needs new thread tape or a new arm seal. These repairs can be urgent if the leak is inside the wall.

A tub drain that is slow or backed up is part of the same bathroom system, and the fix is a cable or a camera, not a new faucet. If the tub and the shower both drain slowly, see our drain cleaning page.

Supply valves and fixture connections

The small shutoff valves under sinks and behind toilets are the first line of defense when a fixture fails. Older valves can seize or leak from the stem, and a broken or burst supply line can flood a cabinet fast. Braided stainless steel supply lines are common replacements, and they should be inspected every few years.

If a supply valve breaks or sprays, close the main valve and call. The repair is quick once water is off. Also consider replacing old valves at the same time, because they tend to fail together.

Faucets, shutoffs and the code

Fixtures and their stop valves are covered by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Every fixture should have a way to shut it off without closing the whole house.

What to do now: turn the stop valve under the sink clockwise, or close the main valve if there is none.
