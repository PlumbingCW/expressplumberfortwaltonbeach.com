About Brooks Street

According to Emerald Coast Magazine, Brooks Street is a tree-lined street close to downtown that holds single-family homes, townhomes and condominiums, some with bay views. The article says the street and the Brooks Bridge are named for John Thomas Brooks, who bought just over 100 acres of what is now downtown. The bridge crosses Highway 98 and connects downtown to Okaloosa Island.

The City of Fort Walton Beach lists Fort Walton Beach Landing Park at 139 Brooks Street SE, a waterfront park with about 700 feet of shoreline on Santa Rosa Sound. An address on Brooks Street SE checked against Census data falls inside the city limits. The street is mixed use, so some blocks are mostly homes and others sit beside businesses.

Townhomes and condos: who fixes what

In a single-family house, everything past the meter is yours. In a townhome or condominium, the line is split between you and an association. A leak inside your unit is usually yours to fix. A leak in a shared riser, a common drain or a roof is usually the association's. Your governing documents set the exact line, so read them or ask your manager before you call anyone.

Speed matters in attached buildings because water travels. A slow leak in one unit can stain a neighbor's ceiling. Know where your unit shutoff is, tell the association if water is leaving your unit and take photos of the damage before cleanup begins.

Plumbing work common in attached homes

These are general topics for townhomes and condos. They are not claims about particular buildings on Brooks Street.

Water heater closets

Tanks often sit in a closet or a small utility space. Check for a drain pan, rust trails or damp flooring. See water heater replacement.

Shared drain lines

If several units back up together, the cause may be a common line. If only your fixtures back up, the cause is likely inside your unit. See drain cleaning.

Leaks behind walls

Water stains on a ceiling or a damp baseboard can come from a unit above. See leak detection.

Fixture upgrades

Faucet, toilet and shutoff valve replacements are common before a sale. See plumbing installation and toilet repair.

If you see water in a condo or townhome

Water in an attached building is time sensitive, so work through these in order.

1

Close your unit shutoff

If you are not sure which valve it is, use the main valve for the unit.

2

Tell your association or manager

Shared systems are their responsibility and they may need to shut off a riser.

3

Protect what you can

Move belongings and switch off power to wet areas only if it is safe.

4

Photograph the damage

Take pictures before cleanup for your insurer and the association.

5

Call us to discuss the repair
