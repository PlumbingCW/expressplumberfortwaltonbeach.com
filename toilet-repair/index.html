When a toilet problem is an emergency

A toilet is an emergency when water is rising over the rim, when it leaks around the base onto the floor or when it will not stop running. Our 24/7 line is open, and the steps below stop the water while you wait.

1

Lift the tank lid and raise the float

Holding the float or flapper chain up stops the tank from refilling.

2

Close the supply valve

Turn the small valve on the wall behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.

3

Do not flush again

A second flush on a blocked toilet adds more water to the bowl.

4

Soak up what spilled

Towels at the base keep water from reaching baseboards and subfloor.

If water is coming up in other fixtures too, the blockage is likely in the main line. Our sewer line cleaning page covers that.

Why a toilet runs, leaks or flushes weakly

Most toilet trouble comes from a handful of inexpensive parts. A worn flapper lets water leak from the tank into the bowl, which makes the fill valve run on and off. A fill valve that sticks can overfill the tank. A cracked tank or loose bolts can drip at the tank-to-bowl joint.

A weak flush is usually partial blockage in the trap or the rim jets, not a broken part. If the toilet flushes slowly but is not clogged, mineral deposits at the rim holes or a short flapper chain are often the cause.

Toilet symptoms and the usual fix

Use the table to match the symptom to the likely repair.

Water drips from the tankLoose tank bolts or cracked tankTighten or replace gaskets, or replace the tank

Weak or slow flushPartial clog or rim jet buildupClear the trap or clean the jets

A toilet that rocks should not be put off. A bad seal lets water seep under the floor and cause rot over time.

Replacing or installing a toilet

Some toilets are worth replacing instead of repairing. A cracked bowl, an older model that uses a lot of water per flush or one with repeated leaks can cost more to fix than to replace. A new toilet is also an easy upgrade in a remodel.

Installation includes removing the old toilet, checking and repairing the flange, setting a new wax ring, aligning the bowl and connecting the supply line. Good installs sit level and do not rock. If the flange is broken, a repair ring or a replacement flange is part of the job.

Toilets in rentals and older Fort Walton Beach homes

Rentals and vacation homes see heavy and varied use, and a toilet that is flushed with the wrong items by a guest can clog without warning. Posting a note about what can go down the toilet cuts these calls noticeably. Wipes sold as flushable are a common cause of blockages in older drain lines.

In homes with older plumbing, the water supply line and shutoff valve behind the toilet may be corroded. A valve that will not turn or leaks at the stem should be replaced at the same time. For a broader look at fixtures, see faucet repair.

Toilet repairs and what to do first

A toilet has to meet fixture rules in the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC), including a secure flange and a sealed base. A rocking toilet is a repair item and not just an annoyance.

What to do now: close the stop valve behind the toilet if it is overflowing, lift the tank float and keep people off a wet floor.
