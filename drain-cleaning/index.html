When a clogged drain is an emergency

A drain turns into an emergency when water is climbing toward the rim, when two or more fixtures slow down at once, or when a toilet or floor drain starts to gurgle. Our line answers 24/7, so a Saturday night backup gets the same attention as a slow sink on a Monday.

While you wait for the plumber, keep the problem from growing.

1

Stop adding water

No flushing, no dishwasher and no laundry. Every gallon you add has to go somewhere.

2

Hold off on chemical cleaner

Caustic products can sit in a blocked line and splash back on whoever opens the pipe next.

3

Move rugs and stored boxes

Pull anything absorbent away from floor drains and low tubs.

4

Note which fixtures are affected

It tells the plumber whether the blockage is local or in the main line.

If the tub gurgles when the washer drains, the blockage is almost always in the main line and not in the tub itself. Say so when you call.

How a clogged drain gets cleared

Most household clogs are cleared with a cable machine, usually called snaking. A cable with a cutting head is fed into the line until it meets the blockage, then it breaks through or hooks the material and pulls it back. Sinks and tubs get a hand-crank or small drum machine, while a main line takes a larger machine and more cable.

When the cable punches a hole but the drain slows again in a few weeks, the pipe wall is usually coated with grease, scale or roots. That is the point where hydro jetting pays off, because high-pressure water scours the whole pipe wall rather than opening a channel down the middle.

If the cable keeps stopping at the same spot, we can follow up with a sewer camera inspection to see whether a crushed or offset pipe is the real problem.

Reading the symptoms: where the clog is sitting

Where water backs up tells you more than the clog itself does. Use the pattern below to describe the problem on the phone.

One sink is slow

A trap or short branch line is partly blocked. This is the easiest clog to clear.

Kitchen sink backs up into the dishwasher side

Grease and food scraps have built up past the garbage disposal or in the sink branch.

Tub and shower back up together

The bathroom branch line is blocked, often by hair and soap film.

Every drain gurgles

Air is being pushed through a partly blocked main line. Expect backups at the lowest fixture first.

Floor drain bubbles or smells

The floor drain shares the main line, so a blockage downstream can push water up through it.

Kitchen sink clogs near the Gulf

Kitchen sinks are where most calls start. Bacon grease, cooking oil, rice and coffee grounds are the usual culprits, and on the Gulf Coast the list also includes shrimp shells and the oily leftovers of a fish fry. Fat cools inside the pipe and sticks, and each meal adds another layer until the opening is the size of a pencil.

If the disposal hums but water drains slowly, the clog is usually past the disposal in the horizontal run to the wall. Our garbage disposal repair page covers jams and failed units, which are a different problem from a clogged pipe.

A garbage disposal grinds food but it does not dissolve grease. Wipe pans with a paper towel before washing and pour cooled oil into a jar for the trash.

What tends to clog drains in Fort Walton Beach homes

Many homes around the Sound sit on sandy soil with large oaks and pines nearby, and roots follow moisture into older clay and cast iron lines. A main line that clogs every spring and fall often has a root intrusion at a joint. Cable work clears it for a season, but the roots regrow until the pipe is cleaned thoroughly or repaired. If that pattern sounds familiar, see our notes on sewer line repair.

Rentals and vacation homes add another cause. A house that sits empty between guests can develop dry traps that let sewer gas rise, and a guest who flushes the wrong things can block a line before the owner knows. Floor drains in garages and laundry rooms need a little water poured in now and then to keep the trap sealed.

Code and what to do first

A drain line has to be sized, sloped and vented to the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Repeated clogs can point to a slope or vent fault and not just a blockage.

What to do while you wait: stop running water into the blocked fixture, keep drain chemicals out of the line and note which fixtures are slow.
