On Demand Hot Water Heater Installation in Fort Walton Beach, FL

An on demand hot water heater, also called a tankless heater, heats water as you use it and has no storage tank. Installing one is a bigger job than swapping a tank, and this guide explains what changes for a home in Fort Walton Beach, FL.

What on demand means in practice

When you open a hot tap, a flow sensor starts the heater and the water is warmed as it flows through. The supply does not run out as long as the unit can keep up with the flow. The limit is how many gallons per minute it can heat to your target temperature.

That limit decides the choice of model. A house where two showers and a dishwasher run together needs a larger unit than a home with one bathroom. Sizing for peak demand is the key, and it is where many installs go wrong.

What changes from a tank install

The table lists the items that usually change when you move from a tank to an on demand unit.

ItemTank heaterOn demand heater
SpaceFloor space for the tankSmall wall-mounted unit
Gas lineStandard house line is usually enoughOften needs a larger line
VentingStandard flue or electricSpecial vent material, often through a wall
ElectricalStandard circuit or gas pilotPower for a gas unit, or a large circuit for an electric one
MaintenanceFlush and anode rodAnnual flush and filter cleaning

A good plumber checks gas pipe size, venting route and electrical capacity before quoting.

Gas or electric

A gas unit delivers more hot water per minute and suits larger homes, but it needs a gas line of the right size and a vent to the outside. An electric unit avoids venting, but whole-house models draw a great deal of power and may call for a panel upgrade.

What the installation day looks like

Plan for a longer visit than a tank swap.

1

Remove the old heater

The tank is drained and disconnected, and the space is cleared.

2

Mount the new unit

The heater is mounted on the wall at the specified height and clearance.

3

Run new gas, water and vent lines

Gas pipe may be upsized and a new vent route is installed.

4

Add service valves

Isolation valves make later flushing simple.

5

Commission and test

The unit is started, temperature and flow are checked and the controls are explained.

Ask the plumber to install service valves. They save money on every future flush.

Is it the right choice for your home

On demand units can save space and avoid standby heat loss, but the savings depend on use. They cost more to buy and install, and they need maintenance. They can be a good fit for a home with limited floor space or for an owner who plans to stay a long time.

If your tank is failing today and you need hot water fast, a tank replacement may be the quicker path. See water heater replacement. You can still upgrade to on demand later.

What an installation visit covers

Before any work starts, a plumber checks the fuel supply, the venting path and the water line sizes. A gas unit needs enough gas flow and a safe exhaust route. An electric unit needs panel space and wire capacity. Skipping this check is how people end up with a unit that cannot keep up.

After the check, you get a plan that names the unit, the venting and any code items. Homeowners in Destin and Shalimar often start with a call and a few photos of the current heater, the gas meter and the panel. Those photos save time on the first visit.

Our tankless water heater page explains the service in more detail.

Plan the old heater removal as well. A tank heater is heavy, and the closet or garage space it leaves behind can hold the new unit on the wall, which frees floor area for storage once the work is done.

A gas or electric tankless unit is installed under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and an electric unit also follows the National Electrical Code (NEC).

Fort Walton Beach local note: permits and codes

Water heater installations in Florida follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, and permit rules vary by project and location. Ask the Fort Walton Beach building department, or the county for homes outside the city, what applies to your address before work begins.

To confirm that any plumber you hire is licensed, the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation lets you look up licenses by name or number at MyFloridaLicense.com. We recommend checking before you sign any contract.
