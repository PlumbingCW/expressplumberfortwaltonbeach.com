Water Softener and Filtration in Fort Walton Beach, FL

Water softener and filtration systems treat water as it enters your house, and the right one depends on what is in your water. The best first step is to test your tap, because guessing leads to buying the wrong system.

Start by testing your water

Water treatment is not one product. A softener removes minerals that cause scale. A sediment filter catches particles. A carbon filter reduces chlorine taste and odor. A reverse osmosis system treats drinking water at one tap. Each solves a different problem.

We do not state a hardness figure for Fort Walton Beach, because it varies by water system and address. The City of Fort Walton Beach describes its supply as groundwater from deep wells in the Floridan Aquifer and publishes an annual water quality report, and its 2020 report does not list hardness. The honest way to know is to test your tap and to ask your water utility for its annual water quality report.

A lab test from a certified water testing lab gives you the numbers. A home test strip is only a rough guide.

What a softener does and its tradeoffs

A softener exchanges calcium and magnesium for sodium or potassium using resin beads, and it regenerates with brine. It reduces scale on fixtures and in water heaters. It also needs salt, a drain connection and attention to settings.

A softener does not make water safe to drink. If your concern is contaminants and not scale, a softener is the wrong tool. Another consideration is wastewater, because regeneration sends brine to a drain, and some areas have rules about discharge to septic systems. Ask before installing.

What an installation includes

Installing a treatment system is a half-day job in most homes.

1

Choose location

A garage or utility area near the main water line and a drain works best.

2

Shut off and tie in

Water is shut off and the system is plumbed in with a bypass.

3

Connect drain and overflow

Brine or backwash lines run to a proper drain.

4

Set controls and test

Settings are programmed and the system is run through a cycle.

Keep the bypass valve handy. It lets you route water around the system for service.

Repairing an existing system

Older water conditioners fail in a few ways. Control valves jam, resin beds exhaust, brine tanks bridge with salt, and drain lines clog. If your softener is not producing soft water, a plumber can test the output, check the salt and the regeneration cycle, and tell you whether the resin needs replacing.

A system that runs constantly or leaks from the control head needs service quickly. Water treatment that is not working properly can waste water and salt. If you are planning a larger plumbing update, our plumbing installation page covers related work.

Treatment equipment and the code

Softeners and filters connect to your supply and drain, so the connection must meet the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Ask for a water test first and match the equipment to the result.

When to call us: if you notice staining, taste or scale and want the cause tested before you buy.
