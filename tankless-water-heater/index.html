Tankless Water Heater Service in Fort Walton Beach, FL

A tankless water heater heats water on demand instead of storing it, so it supplies hot water as long as the flow continues. We install, repair and flush these units, and the flush matters more than most owners expect.

How a tankless water heater works

When a hot tap opens, flow sensors trigger the burner or heating elements and water is heated as it passes through a heat exchanger. There is no storage tank, so the supply does not run out. The limit is how much water the unit can heat at once, which is its flow rate in gallons per minute.

Gas models use a burner and vent, and electric models use heating elements that draw a lot of power. A tankless heater that is too small for the home delivers lukewarm water when several fixtures run at once. Sizing for the highest demand, not the average, is the key.

Flush and descale: the maintenance most owners skip

Mineral scale builds inside the heat exchanger and reduces flow and heating. Manufacturers call for periodic flushing with a descaling solution, and the schedule depends on your water. How fast scale builds varies from home to home, so the honest way to know is to test your tap and to ask your water utility for its annual water quality report.

A flush circulates a mild descaling solution through the unit using a pump and hoses, then rinses it. Many service valves are installed during the original job to make the flush simple. If yours has no service valves, they can be added.

Write down the code on the display before you call. It speeds up the diagnosis.

Gas or electric tankless: what the choice involves

A gas tankless model needs a gas line large enough for the burner, plus venting to the outside. A typical house gas line is often too small for a tankless unit without upgrades. An electric model avoids venting but needs significant electrical capacity, and larger units may need a dedicated circuit and a panel upgrade.

Our water heater installation page walks through the options in more detail. If your tank heater has failed and you want to switch, water heater replacement explains the decision between repair and replacement.

Tankless units and the code

A tankless unit still has to meet the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and a large electric model draws power that must meet the National Electrical Code (NEC). Venting is set by the maker's instructions.

When to call us: if you see an error code, a flame fault or a cold water sandwich, and before you change any settings.
