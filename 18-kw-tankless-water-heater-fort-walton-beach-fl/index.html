18 kW Tankless Water Heater: A Fort Walton Beach, FL Homeowner Guide

An 18 kW electric tankless water heater is a mid-size unit that heats water with large electric elements. Whether it is enough for your home depends on flow and on your electrical panel, and this guide explains both for homeowners in Fort Walton Beach, FL.

What 18 kW means

The kilowatt rating tells you how much electrical power the heater can use at full output. More power means more flow at a given temperature rise, and more current from your panel. An 18 kW unit on a 240 volt supply draws about 75 amps at full load, which is a significant demand.

That draw is why electrical capacity is the first thing to check. Many homes do not have a spare circuit that large, and some do not have the panel capacity for it. An electrician or plumber reviews the panel before a unit is chosen.

What the numbers look like

Heating water takes energy, and the temperature rise you need depends on how cold the incoming water is. The table gives a general sense of what changes when conditions change.

Check the manufacturer sizing chart rather than relying on the kilowatt number alone.

Can one unit run the whole house

An 18 kW unit can serve a small to mid-size home with modest simultaneous use, such as one shower and a small appliance. It struggles when two showers and the dishwasher run together, because the flow is split and the temperature falls.

Larger homes may need a bigger unit, two units or a different fuel. If you have gas and the line is large enough, a gas tankless unit may deliver more flow. Our tankless water heater page explains the maintenance side, and a plumber can review your demand.

Planning the electrical side

The electrical work is the main variable in an electric tankless install.

1

Check the panel capacity

An electrician adds up the loads and confirms spare capacity.

2

Plan the circuit

The unit needs a dedicated double-pole breaker and heavy-gauge wire sized by code.

3

Confirm the wire run

A long distance from the panel affects wire size.

4

Plan the mounting spot

The unit needs clearance and a location near the main hot water lines.

Ask for the electrical and plumbing scope in writing so you can compare quotes fairly.

Other things to know before you buy

Electric tankless units have no stored hot water, so a power outage means no hot water at all. During hurricane season, which runs from June 1 through November 30, that matters. A tank heater can still supply some hot water from its stored volume for a while, though the heating stops.

Running cost depends on local electric rates and on how much hot water you use. If you are replacing a gas tank, compare fuel costs and the install cost carefully. For a planned swap, read water heater installation. A failed tank is covered on our water heater replacement page.

Questions to settle before you buy

Start with your panel. A large electric tankless unit draws heavy current, and some homes need a service upgrade before it can run. Next, count how many fixtures you use at the same time. A shower and a dishwasher together ask a lot of one unit.

Then think about the water. Cold incoming water makes the unit work harder, so the flow you get in winter can be lower than in summer. People in Mary Esther and Navarre who want hot water for a whole house should ask for a sizing check and not rely on the box label alone.

A plumber can add up the load and tell you whether one unit or two will do the job.

Keep the paperwork from the visit, including the load figures and the model chosen. If you later add a bathroom or an outdoor kitchen, that record shows how much headroom the unit has left and whether a second unit makes sense.

A heavy electric load must be wired to the National Electrical Code (NEC), so a licensed electrician or contractor should confirm the panel and circuit before you buy.

Fort Walton Beach local note: permits and licensed work

Electrical and plumbing work for a water heater in Florida follows the Florida Building Code, and permit requirements vary by project and location. Ask the Fort Walton Beach building department, or the county for homes outside the city, what applies to your address.

The Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation provides a license lookup at MyFloridaLicense.com where you can verify any contractor by name or number. Do this for both the plumber and the electrician.
