Emergency Sewer Line Cleaning in Fort Walton Beach, FL

Sewer line cleaning clears the main drain that carries every fixture in the house to the city sewer or septic tank. When that line is blocked, the first sign is often sewage backing up in the lowest tub or floor drain.

Sewer backup: what to do first

A sewer backup is urgent because the water that returns is wastewater. The 24/7 line is open, and these steps limit the damage until a plumber is on the way.

Stop all water use

Do not flush, shower or run the washer. The line is already full.

Keep people out of the area

Children and pets should stay away from the backed-up drain and wet flooring.

Find your cleanout

It is usually a capped pipe in the yard or near the house. Tell the dispatcher where it is.

Photograph the damage

Pictures help if you file an insurance claim later.

Do not open the cleanout cap yourself if sewage is already rising. Pressure can push it out.

How main sewer line cleaning is done

A plumber opens the cleanout and feeds a heavy cable into the main line. The cutting head chews through roots, scale and soft blockages, and the cable is pulled back to clear what remains. For heavier buildup the next tool is hydro jetting, which washes the pipe wall clean.

When no cleanout exists, the plumber may need to pull a toilet or work from a roof vent. That adds time and is one reason a cleanout is worth installing if your house lacks one.

Warning signs of a failing sewer line

A main line rarely fails without warning. These signs are worth acting on before the backup arrives.

Gurgling from toilets and drains

Air is being pushed through a partial blockage.

Slow drains in every bathroom

Several fixtures slowing at once points to the main, not a single branch.

Wet or sunken patches in the yard

A leaking or collapsed pipe can saturate the soil above it.

Sewer smell near the cleanout

Gas escaping from a cracked pipe or loose cap.

Backups after heavy rain

Groundwater may be entering the pipe through a joint or a root gap.

Keep a note of when symptoms appear and what was running, because it helps the plumber narrow the location.

Roots, sand and storm weather

Pine and oak roots work into clay and older cast iron joints looking for water. The soil around the Sound is sandy and drains fast, which keeps roots searching, and a hairline gap at a joint is enough to let them in. Once they have a hold, they trap paper and grease and a partial blockage builds.

Hurricane season runs from June 1 through November 30, and heavy rain can raise the water table around buried lines. A pipe with an open joint can take on groundwater and overload a system that handles normal flow fine. If your backups line up with big storms, say so on the call and ask about a camera look through our sewer inspection service.

Cleanouts and the code

The International Plumbing Code (IPC), on which the Florida Building Code, Plumbing is based, requires cleanouts so a sewer line can be reached for service. Knowing where yours is saves time on any visit.

What to do before we arrive: stop using water, leave the cleanout cap in place and keep the area around it clear.
