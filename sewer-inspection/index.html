Sewer Camera Inspection in Fort Walton Beach, FL

A sewer camera inspection sends a waterproof camera through your main drain line so a plumber can see the pipe from the inside. It turns guesses about roots, cracks and sags into footage you can review.

What a sewer camera inspection shows

A flexible cable with a camera head is fed through a cleanout or an open vent. The camera sends live video to a monitor and the plumber watches for roots, cracks, offset joints, scale and low spots that hold water. A locator can mark the depth and position of the camera on the surface so problems can be mapped to a spot in the yard.

The result is a recording that you can keep. It shows the pipe material, the condition of the joints and anything blocking the flow. If a repair is needed, the footage shows where, and if none is needed, you know the line is sound.

How an inspection visit runs

The visit is short and does not need digging. The usual steps are listed below.

1

Locate the access point

A cleanout is best. Without one, the plumber may pull a toilet.

2

Run the camera

The head travels the full length of the line toward the street or tank.

3

Mark the problems

A locator pinpoints each defect so you know where it sits.

4

Review the footage

You see the recording and discuss whether cleaning or repair is needed.

Ask for a copy of the video. It is useful if you later need to compare condition or share it with an inspector.

When a camera inspection is worth the visit

A camera makes sense when a drain has backed up more than once, when you are buying an older home and the seller cannot say what is under the yard, or before any plan to dig, build or plant near the line. It also helps when a cable cleaner keeps hitting the same obstruction.

Buyers in the Fort Walton Beach area often compare homes of very different ages, from midcentury houses near downtown to newer builds farther out. The pipe material and the condition of the line can be quite different even on one street, so an inspection is a reasonable part of due diligence. Home inspectors do not always run a camera, so it is worth asking.

What the camera can reveal

Each finding has a likely next step. The table shows how the footage translates into action.

Roots at a jointWater is getting in through a gapCutting or jetting, then spot repair
Hairline crackPipe wall has failed locallyMonitor, or line the section
Offset jointPipes have shifted apartSpot repair
Belly or sagWaste pools in a low sectionExcavate and regrade
Scale or greaseBuildup narrows the pipeJetting
Clear, smooth pipeLine is in good shapeNo work needed

A clear line is also a useful result. It means a repeat backup is more likely a household habit than a pipe defect.

Cleaning before the camera, or the camera first

Sometimes the line is too full to see through. In that case the plumber clears it first, using a cable or sewer line cleaning methods, then runs the camera so the footage is useful. If the visit starts with an active backup, the cleaning comes first and the camera follows.

Where footage reveals a damaged pipe, the next step is a repair plan. Our sewer line repair page explains the options, and a hydro jetting visit is the usual answer when the pipe is sound but coated.

What an inspection record is good for

A camera inspection is a record of the pipe, not a code approval. A buyer, an insurer or a building department may still ask for their own review under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

What to do before we arrive: locate the cleanout and note any gurgling or slow fixtures.
