High Water Bill but No Visible Leak: A Fort Walton Beach, FL Checklist

A water bill that jumps without a change in habits usually means water is leaking somewhere you cannot see. This checklist helps you narrow it down in an afternoon, so you know whether you need a leak detection visit in Fort Walton Beach, FL.

Start with the bill itself

Compare this bill to the same month last year. A jump of a few percent is normal. A doubling is not. Check the usage figure and the dates on the bill so you are comparing like with like, and see whether the utility flagged unusual use.

If you had guests, a new irrigation schedule or a pool fill, usage will rise for good reasons. If not, move to the meter, because it shows what the house is doing in real time.

The meter check

The meter is your best evidence. This version takes about two hours and no tools beyond a pen.

1

Stop all water use

Close taps, stop the dishwasher and washer and turn off the ice maker.

2

Find and open the meter box

It is usually near the street. Lift the lid carefully and watch for insects.

3

Look at the leak indicator

Most meters have a small triangle or dial that spins when any water moves.

4

Record the reading

Write the numbers and wait two hours with no water use.

5

Compare

If the reading changed, water is leaking on your side of the meter.

If the indicator is still, the high bill may come from usage you did not notice, or from a meter or billing issue. Ask the utility to verify.

Where the water goes when the meter moves

If the meter moved, narrow it down in the order below. Each check rules out a part of the system.

CheckHowWhat it tells you
ToiletsDrop dye in each tank and wait 15 minutes without flushingColor in the bowl means a leaking flapper
IrrigationClose the sprinkler supply valve and recheck the meterIf the meter stops, the system is leaking
Water heaterLook for a wet base and a dripping relief pipeDripping means a failing valve or tank
Faucets and hose bibsListen and look under sinks and outdoorsDrips and wet cabinets are easy to spot
Main valve at the houseClose it and watch the meterIf the meter still moves, the leak is between the meter and the valve

Toilet flappers are the number one cause of high bills with no visible water. Test them first.

If you still cannot find it

Leaks can hide in places that are hard to test. A pipe under a slab, a line behind a wall or a buried service line can leak for weeks. A warm patch on the floor, a musty smell or a wet spot in the yard all give clues.

A plumber with acoustic and thermal equipment can locate the leak without opening up the house. Our leak detection page explains how that works. If the leak is under the concrete, see slab leak repair for what happens next.

Prevent the next surprise

A few habits keep bills predictable. Read the meter once a month and write the number down. Learn where your shutoffs are. Replace old toilet flappers every few years, and have irrigation checked at the start of each season.

If you manage a rental or a second home, a smart water monitor that sends alerts can catch a leak within hours. The people who pay the most for leaks are the ones who find out last.

What to do with the results

If the meter test shows movement with every fixture off, you have a leak and the next job is to locate it. Start with the toilets, then the irrigation system, then the water heater and the line from the meter to the house. A dye tablet in each tank finds a silent toilet leak in minutes.

If the meter stays still, the bill may reflect real use, a seasonal change or an estimated read. Compare the dates on the bill with your own habits and call your utility if the numbers look wrong. Homeowners in Destin and Mary Esther with guests or a pool fill often find that the answer is use and not a leak.

When the cause stays hidden, our leak detection page explains how a listening visit finds it.

Standards for meters and billing are set by your utility, and plumbing work that follows a repair is covered by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Fort Walton Beach local note: your utility and your bill

Water service in the Fort Walton Beach area comes from more than one provider. The City of Fort Walton Beach runs a municipal water system, and Okaloosa County Water and Sewer serves areas around the city, including Shalimar, Okaloosa Island and unincorporated parts near the city. Your bill names your provider, and its customer service line can explain unusual usage and tell you how to request a meter check.

Each utility publishes an annual water quality report. If you want to understand your water, read it and test your tap. We do not state any hardness figure for the area.
