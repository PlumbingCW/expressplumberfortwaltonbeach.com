Emergency Hydro Jetting in Fort Walton Beach, FL

Hydro jetting uses a hose and a steel nozzle to blast water against the inside of a drain or sewer pipe, stripping off grease, scale and roots instead of poking a hole through them. When a line backs up again and again, it is the fix that reaches the pipe wall.

When a backed-up line cannot wait

A backup that sends water into a tub, shower or floor drain is an emergency because the water is wastewater and it is heading for your floors. The 24/7 line is open at night and on weekends, and a plumber can assess whether jetting is the right tool or whether a cable will do.

Until someone arrives, stop running water, keep children and pets away from the affected drain and do not pour chemicals in. Jetting equipment needs a clean access point, so if you know where your cleanout is, tell the dispatcher.

How jetting works and why it lasts longer

A jetting machine pumps water at pressures measured in thousands of pounds per square inch through a hose that ends in a nozzle with rear-facing ports. The jets pull the hose forward and scour the pipe as it goes. Pulling it back out washes the loosened debris toward the cleanout or the main.

A cable cleaner opens a channel through the middle of a clog and leaves the coating on the walls. Hydro jetting removes that coating, which is why it tends to hold for longer when the cause is grease or mineral scale. The tradeoff is that a jetter should not be used on a pipe that is already cracked or collapsed, which is one reason we look first.

What a jetting visit looks like

The process is short and predictable. Here is how a visit usually runs.

1

Find the access point

The plumber locates a cleanout, or pulls a toilet when no cleanout exists.

2

Look inside first

A camera run confirms the pipe is sound enough to jet and shows what is blocking it.

3

Jet the line

The nozzle works from the cleanout toward the main until the water runs clear.

4

Check the result

A second camera pass confirms the walls are clean and shows any damage worth repairing.

Footage from the camera gives you a record of the pipe condition, which is useful for a home inspection or a repair decision later.

Jetting or cable: how to choose

Neither tool is better in every case. The right choice depends on what the line is carrying.

For a drain that has clogged more than once this year, a jetting visit often costs less than the repeat cable calls.

Where jetting helps in Fort Walton Beach

Older neighborhoods near downtown have clay and cast iron lines that scale up over decades, and newer subdivisions have PVC that stays smooth but collects grease at bends. Roots from pines and live oaks are a regular visitor in either one. Restaurants along US 98 and the Okaloosa Island tourist strip deal with fat in their lines every week, which is why many of them schedule jetting instead of waiting for a backup.

If you run a food business, our commercial plumbing page covers grease trap care and after-hours service. For homes, the usual next step after jetting is the camera inspection so you know what the pipe looks like.

Where jetting fits under the code

Jetting cleans the pipe you have and does not change its layout, so the line still has to meet the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). A camera pass tells you whether the pipe is sound enough to jet.

What to do before we arrive: clear access to the cleanout and keep people and pets away from the work area.
